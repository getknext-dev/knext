Replay `'use cache'` invalidations that could not reach Redis. While Redis is unreachable, a `revalidateTag` or `revalidatePath` could not record its invalidation for `'use cache'` entries, so after Redis came back every pod kept returning those entries until they expired (up to 24 hours). The pod now remembers those tags (up to 1,000, deduplicated, shared by the `default` and `remote` kinds) and, as soon as Redis answers again, records the invalidation in the background, timed at that moment so it is never earlier than the call. It never undoes a newer invalidation of the same tag that reached Redis first (from another pod, or a later call on the same pod), and it still lands on a Redis that refuses Lua scripts. If more than 1,000 tags pile up, it removes every `'use cache'` entry of the app instead. The list lives in the pod's memory, so a pod that restarts or scales to zero before Redis is back still leaves those entries in place until they expire. Two new `knext_use_cache_events_total` events, `tags_replayed` and `tags_collapsed`, count the replay; `tags_dropped` now also counts a tag write that failed part-way.
