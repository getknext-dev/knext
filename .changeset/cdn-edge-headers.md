Apps with `cdn` set now send cache-correct headers for the CDN in front of them. On Cloudflare and Google Cloud CDN the edge directive goes in `Cloudflare-CDN-Cache-Control` or `CDN-Cache-Control`, so the browser-facing `Cache-Control` stays exactly what it is today; on CloudFront and Azure Front Door it goes in `Cache-Control` as `s-maxage`. Cacheable pages also carry a `Cache-Tag` header made of fixed-length hashes, so a later purge can target one page, one tag or the whole app. A response is edge-cacheable only when every check passes: a GET or HEAD with status 200 that Next itself marked shared-cacheable, with no `private` or `no-store`, no `Set-Cookie`, no `Authorization` header or draft-mode cookie, no `_rsc` or `RSC` mismatch, a path that is not under `/api/cache/` or your `excludePaths`, and tags recorded for the page. Anything else is left uncached, and nothing is cached when the build artifact that lists your middleware matchers is missing or corrupt. Paths your middleware (or `proxy`) matches are never edge-cached unless you list them in `includePaths`, because a CDN ignores cookies and would otherwise serve a protected page to anonymous visitors; listing a path removes that protection for it. Only request paths in canonical form are cached: paths with `//`, dot segments, a backslash, or percent-encoded `.`, `/`, `\`, `%` or letters and digits always go to the origin. The build now writes `knext-cdn-routing.json` into the build output and the compiled single executable embeds it. Without `cdn` nothing changes: the response headers are byte-identical. When `cdn` is set, `KNEXT_CACHE_CONTROL_NORMALIZE=0` is ignored with one log line; the switch is deprecated in favour of `cdn`. Tag purge on `revalidateTag` and `revalidatePath` is a separate change; until it lands, staleness is bounded by `htmlTTLSeconds` (default 30 seconds).
