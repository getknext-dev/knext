The response-header plumbing for a CDN in front of your app is in place, but **nothing is edge-cached yet**: a page only gets an edge directive and a `Cache-Tag` once the cache handler records its tags, and that change lands separately. Until then every response keeps today's headers. With `cdn` set, the runtime is ready to send the edge directive in `Cloudflare-CDN-Cache-Control` or `CDN-Cache-Control` (so the browser-facing `Cache-Control` stays exactly what it is today), or in `Cache-Control` as `s-maxage` on CloudFront and Azure Front Door, plus a `Cache-Tag` header made of fixed-length hashes. A response will be edge-cacheable only when every check passes: a GET or HEAD with status 200 that Next itself marked shared-cacheable, with no `private` or `no-store`, no `Set-Cookie`, no `Authorization` header or draft-mode cookie, no `_rsc` or `RSC` mismatch, a path that is not under `/api/cache/` or your `excludePaths` (matched with the base path and locale prefix removed as well as raw, and also against the page a `/_next/data/...json` URL stands for), a resolvable build id, and tags recorded for the page. Anything else is left uncached, and nothing is cached when the build artifact that lists your middleware matchers is missing or corrupt. Paths your middleware (or `proxy`) matches are never edge-cached unless you list them in `includePaths`, and neither are paths matched by a `next.config` rewrite, redirect or header rule that depends on a cookie, header, query or host (`has` or `missing`), or by an unconditional rewrite that leads into one, because a CDN ignores those and would otherwise serve one visitor's page to another; listing a path in `includePaths` removes that protection for it. Only request paths in canonical form are cached: paths with `//`, dot segments, a backslash, or percent-encoded `.`, `/`, `\`, `%` or letters and digits always go to the origin. The build now writes `knext-cdn-routing.json` into the build output and the compiled single executable embeds it. Without `cdn` nothing changes: the response headers are byte-identical. When `cdn` is set, `KNEXT_CACHE_CONTROL_NORMALIZE=0` is ignored with one log line; the switch is deprecated in favour of `cdn`. Tag purge on `revalidateTag` and `revalidatePath` is a separate change; until it lands, staleness is bounded by `htmlTTLSeconds` (default 30 seconds).
